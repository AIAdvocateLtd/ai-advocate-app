**R-01** — et_time_limits

Employment tribunal time limits (England, Wales, Scotland) depend on the DATE OF THE ACT (for unfair dismissal, the effective date of termination), not today's date: on or after 1 October 2026 most claims have 6 months less one day; before 1 October 2026, 3 months less one day (statutory redundancy pay and equal pay already had 6 months). The change (Employment Rights Act 2025 s.152 and Sch.12) was commenced on 1 October 2026 by SI 2026/954, which does not apply where the relevant date is before 1 October 2026. Exception: breach of contract claims in Scotland — 6 months only for a breach on or after 9 November 2026 (Acas). Conduct extending over a period runs from the end of the period (Equality Act 2010 s.123(3)); a series of deductions runs from the last deduction (Employment Rights Act 1996 s.23(3)). Acas early conciliation must be started within the limit and pauses it. Never present 6 months as universal, and never tell someone they are definitely out of time.

Sources: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/954, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/36/schedule/12, https://www.acas.org.uk/employment-tribunal-time-limits

**R-02** — ni_employment

**R-03** — wales_tenancy

Wales: since 1 December 2022 the Renting Homes (Wales) Act 2016 governs most rentals — tenants are 'contract-holders' with occupation contracts, and a Housing Act 1988 section 21 notice does not apply to them. The no-fault route is a section 173 notice: for a periodic standard contract agreed on or after 1 December 2022 it must give at least 6 months' notice and cannot be given in the first 6 months of occupation; it cannot be given during a fixed term. Contracts that converted on 1 December 2022 had different transitional rules. A notice is not an eviction — the landlord needs a court possession order, and some breaches by the landlord (for example written-statement or deposit rules) can prevent a valid notice. If a Welsh customer mentions 'section 21', explain this and ask for the notice wording and date; never apply England's section 21 or Renters' Rights Act 2025 rules in Wales. Signpost Shelter Cymru and gov.wales.

Sources: https://www.gov.wales/renting-homes-frequently-asked-questions-tenants, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2016/1/section/173

**R-05** — immigration_core

Immigration (UK-wide): an ordinary visitor visa refusal carries NO right of appeal — appeals exist only where a human rights or protection claim was refused; otherwise the routes are a fresh application or, if the decision was unlawful, judicial review (apply promptly). Treat a document that claims an appeal right for a visitor refusal with caution. ILR continuous residence is usually broken by more than 180 days' absence in any rolling 12-month period (not calendar year), with limited exceptions for serious or compelling reasons. If someone is detained and facing removal within hours, the first step is an immigration solicitor tonight (legal aid may be available; urgent judicial review/injunction applications can be made out of hours) and charities such as Bail for Immigration Detainees — never promise removal can be stopped.

Sources: https://www.gov.uk/immigration-asylum-tribunal, https://www.gov.uk/guidance/immigration-rules/immigration-rules-appendix-continuous-residence, https://www.gov.uk/guidance/apply-for-a-judicial-review-in-an-immigration-or-asylum-case

**R-06** — settlement_agreement

Settlement agreements (GB): a settlement agreement only validly waives statutory employment claims if it is in writing, relates to the particular complaints, and the employee has received advice from a named relevant independent adviser (e.g. a qualified lawyer) with insurance (Employment Rights Act 1996 s.203(3)). Employers commonly contribute to the adviser's fee. Never tell the customer to sign or not sign.
