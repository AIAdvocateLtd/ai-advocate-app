Employment rights in the UK: your rights at work explained

Problems with pay, holiday, sickness, your boss or your job itself? Here's what the law gives you and what to do when it isn't being followed.

Last reviewed: October 2026 · Updated for the Employment Rights Act 2025 · Covers England, Wales and Scotland (Northern Ireland has its own rules)
Quick answer: almost all workers have the right to the minimum wage, 5.6 weeks' paid holiday, rest breaks, payslips, a written statement of terms from day one, sick pay and protection from discrimination. If something's wrong, raise a written grievance and talk to ACAS. Tribunal deadlines are short, so act quickly.

Are you an employee, worker or self-employed?

Your rights depend on your status. Employees get the full set, including unfair dismissal and redundancy pay. Workers (many gig and casual jobs) get core rights such as minimum wage, holiday and discrimination protection. Genuinely self-employed people get very few. Your contract's label isn't decisive; what happens in practice is.

Pay

  • The National Minimum Wage / National Living Wage goes up every April. Check GOV.UK for the current rates.
  • You're entitled to an itemised payslip.
  • Deductions from wages must be allowed by your contract or by law. Unlawful deductions can be claimed back at a tribunal.

Holiday and hours

  • 5.6 weeks' paid holiday a year (28 days for a 5-day week), pro rata for part-time.
  • A maximum 48-hour average working week unless you opt out in writing.
  • A 20-minute break if you work more than 6 hours, and daily and weekly rest.

Sickness

Statutory Sick Pay (SSP) is the legal minimum. Under the Employment Rights Act 2025, from April 2026 SSP is payable from the first day of sickness, and the lower earnings limit has been removed. Your contract may give more.

Family and flexible working

You have rights to maternity, paternity, adoption, shared parental and parental leave, time off for dependants, and to request flexible working. It's unlawful to treat you badly for using these rights.

Discrimination and harassment

The Equality Act 2010 protects you from day one against discrimination, harassment and victimisation. See our workplace discrimination guide.

Losing your job

  • Notice: at least 1 week after a month's service, then 1 week per complete year up to 12 weeks.
  • Unfair dismissal: see our unfair dismissal guide.
  • Redundancy: see our redundancy pay guide.

What to do if your rights are being broken

  1. Keep records: contract, payslips, rotas, emails, messages and notes of conversations.
  2. Raise it informally, then put a formal written grievance in.
  3. Call ACAS for free advice.
  4. If it isn't resolved, start ACAS early conciliation before the tribunal deadline.

Frequently asked questions

What are my basic employment rights?

Minimum wage, 5.6 weeks' paid holiday, rest breaks, payslips, a written statement, sick pay and protection from discrimination, plus dismissal and redundancy rights for employees.

How much holiday do I get?

5.6 weeks a year (28 days for a full-time 5-day week), pro rata for part-time.

Can my employer change my contract?

Not usually without your agreement. Under the Employment Rights Act 2025, "fire and rehire" to force changes is being heavily restricted. Get advice before agreeing.

Who can I contact for help?

ACAS, Citizens Advice, your trade union, or an employment solicitor.
