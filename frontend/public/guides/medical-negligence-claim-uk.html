Medical negligence claims in the UK: time limits and first steps

If NHS or private treatment went wrong and left you worse off, you may be able to claim compensation. Here is how it works, in plain English.

Last reviewed: October 2026 · Covers England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland
Quick answer: you normally have 3 years to start a claim, counted from the date of the treatment or from when you first realised it harmed you. You must show the care fell below an acceptable standard and that this caused your injury. Get your medical records early and speak to a clinical negligence solicitor — many work on a "no win, no fee" basis.

What counts as medical negligence?

Medical (or "clinical") negligence is when a doctor, nurse, dentist, pharmacist or other healthcare professional gives care that falls below the standard of a reasonably competent professional, and you are injured as a result. Common examples include:

  • A missed or delayed diagnosis (for example cancer, sepsis or a fracture)
  • Surgical mistakes or problems that weren't properly explained beforehand
  • Wrong medication or the wrong dose
  • Birth injuries to a mother or baby
  • Failing to get your informed consent to treatment

A bad outcome on its own is not negligence. Some treatments carry risks even when done properly. The question is whether the care itself was below standard.

What you have to prove

  1. Breach of duty: the care fell below what a responsible body of professionals in that field would accept.
  2. Causation: that failure caused your injury, or made it worse, on the balance of probabilities.

Both points are usually decided with the help of independent medical experts, which is why these claims need a specialist solicitor.

Time limits

  • England & Wales: 3 years from the date of negligence, or from your "date of knowledge" — when you first knew you'd been harmed and that it might be due to your care (Limitation Act 1980).
  • Scotland: generally 3 years (Prescription and Limitation (Scotland) Act 1973).
  • Northern Ireland: generally 3 years (Limitation (Northern Ireland) Order 1989).
  • Children: the 3 years usually starts on their 18th birthday, but a parent can claim on their behalf sooner.
  • Lack of mental capacity: the time limit does not run while the person lacks capacity.
  • Someone has died: different rules apply (usually 3 years from the date of death or date of knowledge). Speak to a solicitor quickly.

Don't leave it late — a solicitor needs time to get records and expert reports before the deadline.

What to do first

  1. Get your medical records. Make a subject access request to the hospital or GP practice. It's normally free, and they should reply within one month.
  2. Write down what happened while you remember it: dates, names, what you were told, and how the injury affects your daily life and work.
  3. Keep evidence of your losses: lost earnings, travel to appointments, prescriptions, care from family.
  4. Consider a complaint. For the NHS, use the complaints procedure (or PALS in England) — normally within 12 months. If you're unhappy with the answer, you can go to the Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman (England), the Public Services Ombudsman for Wales, the Scottish Public Services Ombudsman or the NI Public Services Ombudsman. A complaint does not pause the 3-year legal time limit.
  5. Speak to a clinical negligence solicitor. Look for one accredited by the Law Society or a member of AvMA's panel. Many offer a free first assessment.

How much does it cost? "No win, no fee"

Most claims are funded by a conditional fee agreement ("no win, no fee"). If you lose, you usually pay nothing to your own solicitor. If you win, the solicitor can take a success fee from your compensation — in England and Wales this is capped at 25% of your damages (excluding future losses). Always ask for the fee terms in writing before you sign.

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to make a medical negligence claim?

Usually 3 years from the date of the negligence or from when you first knew you'd been harmed by it. For children it starts at 18; it doesn't run while someone lacks mental capacity.

Do I need to complain to the NHS before claiming?

No. A complaint can give you answers and an apology, but it isn't required and it doesn't stop the legal time limit.

Can I get my medical records for free?

Yes — make a subject access request. It's normally free and should be answered within one month.

Will claiming against the NHS affect my treatment?

No. Making a claim should not affect the care you receive. Claims against NHS trusts in England are handled by NHS Resolution, not your doctors.
