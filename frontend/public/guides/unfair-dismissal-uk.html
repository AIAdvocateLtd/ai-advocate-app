Unfair dismissal in the UK: your rights and time limits

Lost your job and think it wasn't fair? Here's who can claim, the deadline you can't miss, and the steps to take.

Last reviewed: October 2026 · Updated for the Employment Rights Act 2025 · Covers England, Wales and Scotland (Northern Ireland has its own rules)
Quick answer: if you were dismissed on or after 1 October 2026, you usually have 6 months less one day to act (before that date it was 3 months less one day), and you must contact ACAS early conciliation first. You need 2 years' service to claim ordinary unfair dismissal, falling to 6 months for dismissals from 1 January 2027. There's no minimum service if the reason was "automatically unfair" (like whistleblowing or pregnancy).

When is a dismissal unfair?

Your employer must have a fair reason and follow a fair process. The potentially fair reasons are conduct, capability (including performance and ill health), redundancy, a legal restriction, or "some other substantial reason". Even with a fair reason, a dismissal can be unfair if your employer:

  • didn't investigate properly or give you a chance to respond
  • didn't follow the ACAS Code of Practice on disciplinary and grievance procedures
  • didn't let you be accompanied to a disciplinary meeting or give you a right of appeal
  • treated you more harshly than others in a similar situation

Automatically unfair reasons (no minimum service)

  • Pregnancy, maternity, paternity or adoption leave
  • Whistleblowing (reporting wrongdoing in the public interest)
  • Asking for a legal right, such as the minimum wage or holiday pay
  • Trade union membership or activity
  • Raising health and safety concerns

If you were treated badly because of a protected characteristic (such as race, sex, disability, age or religion), you may also have a discrimination claim, which doesn't need any minimum service.

Who can claim: the qualifying period

You need to be an employee (not self-employed). Under the Employment Rights Act 2025:

  • If your employment ends before 1 January 2027, you need 2 years' continuous employment.
  • If it ends on or after 1 January 2027, you need just 6 months.

These changes apply in England, Wales and Scotland. Northern Ireland has its own employment rules.

The time limit and ACAS early conciliation

  1. Note your "effective date of termination", normally your last day of employment.
  2. Contact ACAS early conciliation within the time limit: 6 months less one day if you were dismissed on or after 1 October 2026, or 3 months less one day for earlier dismissals. It's free, required before a tribunal claim, and pauses the clock while ACAS tries to help you settle.
  3. Get your certificate. If it doesn't settle, ACAS gives you an early conciliation certificate number.
  4. Submit your ET1 claim form to the employment tribunal before the extended deadline. Late claims are very rarely accepted.

What to do right now

  • Appeal internally if your employer offers an appeal, but don't let it run past your deadline.
  • Keep everything: dismissal letter, contract, emails, messages, payslips and notes of meetings.
  • Write a timeline of what happened while it's fresh.
  • Ask for written reasons for your dismissal.

What can you get?

If you win, the tribunal can order reinstatement, but most people get compensation: a basic award (based on age, service and weekly pay, up to a cap) and a compensatory award for lost earnings. The caps are updated every April. Many claims settle through ACAS before a hearing.

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to claim unfair dismissal?

6 months less one day if you were dismissed on or after 1 October 2026 (3 months less one day before that), and you must start ACAS early conciliation first.

Do I need 2 years' service?

Yes if your employment ends before 1 January 2027; from that date it's 6 months. No minimum service is needed for automatically unfair reasons or discrimination.

Does a tribunal cost money?

There's currently no fee to bring a claim, and each side normally pays its own legal costs.

Can I claim if I resigned?

Possibly. If your employer seriously breached your contract and you resigned because of it, you may have a "constructive dismissal" claim. These are harder to win, so get advice first.
