Lawyers near me: how to find a good solicitor in the UK

"I need a lawyer" is a stressful thought. Here's how to find the right one quickly, check they're genuine, keep costs down, and work out whether you need one at all.

Last reviewed: October 2026 · Covers England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland
Quick answer: search the Law Society's Find a Solicitor directory by postcode and area of law, check the firm on the SRA register, and ask for a free first consultation and a written cost estimate. For many problems, such as deposits, refunds and ACAS claims, you can start on your own before paying anyone.

Do you actually need a lawyer?

You usually should get a solicitor for: criminal charges, buying or selling a home, divorce with money or children in dispute, serious injury, immigration appeals, and high-value contracts.

You can often start yourself for: deposit disputes, refunds and small claims, parking tickets, flight compensation and starting ACAS early conciliation.

Step 1: Search the official directories

  • England and Wales — Law Society "Find a Solicitor"
  • Scotland — Law Society of Scotland "Find a Solicitor"
  • Northern Ireland — Law Society of Northern Ireland directory
  • Legal Aid — GOV.UK "Find a legal aid adviser or family mediator"

Filter by your postcode and the area of law. A specialist (for example "employment" or "family") usually costs less overall than a generalist, because they work faster.

Step 2: Check they're genuine

  • Look them up on the SRA register (England and Wales).
  • Look for the clickable SRA digital badge on their website.
  • Be wary of anyone who pressures you to pay quickly or changes bank details by email.

Step 3: Understand the cost

  • Fixed fee — common for wills, conveyancing, uncontested divorce.
  • Hourly rate — ask for an estimate and a cap.
  • No win, no fee — common for personal injury and medical negligence.
  • Legal Aid — free if you qualify. See our Legal Aid guide.

Step 4: Questions to ask at the first meeting

  1. Have you handled cases like mine? What happened?
  2. Who will actually do the work, and what's their hourly rate?
  3. What's the total likely cost, and what could make it go up?
  4. What are my chances, and are there deadlines I must meet?
  5. Is there a cheaper way to resolve this?

Step 5: Go prepared

Solicitors charge for time, so arrive with a short summary, a timeline of events, and copies of key documents. That alone can save you hundreds of pounds.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find a solicitor near me?

Use the Law Society's Find a Solicitor tool (or the Scottish/NI equivalents), search by postcode and area of law, then check the firm on the SRA register.

How do I check a solicitor is real?

Search the SRA register and look for the SRA digital badge. Confirm bank details by phone before paying.

What's the difference between a solicitor and a barrister?

Solicitors handle your case day to day and give advice. Barristers specialise in court advocacy and specialist opinions, and are usually instructed by a solicitor, although some accept direct public access.

Can I complain about a solicitor?

Complain to the firm first. If you're unhappy with the outcome, go to the Legal Ombudsman (service) or the SRA (conduct).
