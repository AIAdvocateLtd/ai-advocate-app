Redundancy pay in the UK: your rights and entitlement

Told your job is at risk? Here's what you're owed, how the process should work, and how to spot an unfair redundancy.

Last reviewed: October 2026 · Covers England, Wales and Scotland (Northern Ireland has similar but separate rules)
Quick answer: if you're an employee with 2+ years' service, you're entitled to statutory redundancy pay: ½, 1 or 1½ weeks' pay per full year depending on your age, up to 20 years and subject to a weekly pay cap. You're also owed notice pay and any unused holiday. Your employer must consult you and fairly select who goes.

How statutory redundancy pay is worked out

  • ½ week's pay for each full year worked while under 22
  • 1 week's pay for each full year aged 22 to 40
  • 1½ weeks' pay for each full year aged 41 or over

Only the last 20 years count, and weekly pay is capped (the cap rises every April; check GOV.UK's redundancy pay calculator). Your contract may promise more ("enhanced" redundancy pay).

What else you're owed

  • Notice pay: at least 1 week per full year of service, up to 12 weeks, or more if your contract says so.
  • Holiday pay for any unused leave.
  • Reasonable paid time off to look for work during your notice (after 2 years' service).

Is it a genuine redundancy?

Redundancy means the business is closing, your workplace is closing, or fewer people are needed to do your kind of work. If your role is quickly re-advertised or someone else is simply doing your job, it may not be genuine.

A fair redundancy process

  1. Consultation with you individually (and collectively if 20+ redundancies are proposed at one site within 90 days).
  2. Fair selection criteria applied objectively, such as skills, performance or disciplinary records.
  3. Considering suitable alternative work, with a 4-week trial period if you accept a new role.
  4. A right to appeal.

Selecting you because of pregnancy, maternity leave, age, disability, whistleblowing or union activity is unlawful. See workplace discrimination.

Time limits

If you think the redundancy was unfair, the unfair dismissal time limits and ACAS early conciliation apply. For an unpaid redundancy payment, you normally have 6 months to claim. If your employer has gone bust, apply to the Redundancy Payments Service.

Frequently asked questions

Who gets redundancy pay?

Employees with 2+ years' continuous service who are dismissed because of redundancy.

Is redundancy pay taxed?

The first £30,000 of a genuine redundancy payment is usually tax-free. Notice pay and holiday pay are taxed as normal.

Can I volunteer for redundancy?

Yes, if your employer asks for volunteers. You still get statutory redundancy pay if you qualify.

Should I sign a settlement agreement?

You must get independent legal advice before it's valid, and employers usually contribute to the cost of that advice. Don't sign until you understand what you're giving up.
