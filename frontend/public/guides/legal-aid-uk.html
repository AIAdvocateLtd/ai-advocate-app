Can I get Legal Aid? What it covers and who qualifies

Legal Aid pays for a solicitor if you can't afford one, but only for certain problems. Here's how to tell quickly whether you're likely to qualify.

Last reviewed: October 2026 · Mainly covers England and Wales (Scotland and Northern Ireland run their own schemes)
Quick answer: civil Legal Aid mostly covers housing and eviction, family cases with domestic abuse or child protection, discrimination, asylum and immigration, mental health, and debt where your home is at risk. You'll usually need to pass a means test and a merits test. Advice at a police station is free for everyone.

What Legal Aid usually covers

  • Housing — eviction, homelessness, serious disrepair that risks your health
  • Family — if you or your children have been victims of domestic abuse, or children may be taken into care; family mediation
  • Debt — if your home is at risk (repossession, bankruptcy affecting your home)
  • Discrimination under the Equality Act 2010
  • Immigration and asylum — asylum, trafficking, some detention cases
  • Mental health and community care
  • Welfare benefits — appeals on a point of law to the Upper Tribunal and higher courts
  • Criminal — police station advice, and court representation if it's in the interests of justice

What it usually doesn't cover

Most employment claims (other than discrimination), consumer disputes, personal injury, wills, conveyancing and most divorces without domestic abuse. For these, look at free advice options, insurance or no win, no fee.

The means test

This looks at your household income, savings and assets (including equity in your home). If you receive certain benefits, such as Universal Credit, you may automatically pass the income part. Domestic abuse victims can get some tests waived for protective orders.

The merits test

Your case must have a reasonable chance of success and be worth pursuing. The solicitor assesses this for you.

How to apply

  1. Use the GOV.UK "Check if you can get legal aid" tool.
  2. Find a provider with GOV.UK "Find a legal aid adviser", or call Civil Legal Advice.
  3. Bring proof of income, benefits, savings and any documents about your problem.
  4. The solicitor applies to the Legal Aid Agency for you.

Frequently asked questions

What does Legal Aid cover?

Mainly housing, family cases involving abuse or child protection, discrimination, immigration and asylum, mental health, debt where your home is at risk, and criminal cases.

Who qualifies for Legal Aid?

People who pass the means and merits tests. Some benefits passport you through the income test.

Will I have to pay Legal Aid back?

Sometimes. If you win money or property, the "statutory charge" may mean some of your legal costs come out of it. Ask your solicitor at the start.

What if I don't qualify?

Try Citizens Advice, a Law Centre, pro bono clinics, legal expenses insurance, or fixed-fee help from a solicitor.
