Selling your home: the legal steps explained

From accepting an offer to handing over the keys, here's what happens legally when you sell a house or flat in England or Wales, what you need to do at each step, and how to avoid the delays that make sales fall through.

Last reviewed: October 2026 · Covers England and Wales (Scotland uses a different system)
Quick answer: instruct a conveyancer as soon as you put your home on the market, complete the TA6 and TA10 forms honestly, answer the buyer's enquiries quickly, then exchange contracts (when the sale becomes binding) and complete (when the money arrives and you hand over the keys). It usually takes 12–20 weeks.

1. Before you accept an offer

  • Get a valid Energy Performance Certificate (EPC). You need one before marketing.
  • Instruct a conveyancer early. They'll do ID checks and order your title deeds now, saving weeks later.
  • Gather documents: building regulations sign-offs, planning permissions, FENSA certificates, gas and electrical certificates, and guarantees.

2. Accepting an offer

Your estate agent sends a memorandum of sale to both conveyancers. Nothing is legally binding yet, so either side can still pull out.

3. The seller's property forms

You complete the TA6 Property Information Form (boundaries, disputes, alterations, flooding, services and more) and the TA10 Fittings and Contents Form (what's staying and going). Leasehold sellers also complete the TA7.

Honesty matters. If you give a wrong answer, or hide a known problem like a past neighbour dispute or flooding, the buyer can sue you after the sale for misrepresentation. If you don't know, say "not known" rather than guessing.

4. Contract pack and enquiries

Your conveyancer sends the draft contract, title documents and your forms to the buyer's conveyancer. They review it with their searches and send enquiries (questions). Answering these quickly and accurately is the single biggest thing you can do to speed up your sale.

5. Signing and agreeing a completion date

You sign the contract and the transfer deed (TR1), usually with a witness. Everyone in the chain agrees a completion date.

6. Exchange of contracts

The sale becomes legally binding. The buyer pays a deposit (often 10%), and the completion date is fixed. Keep your buildings insurance until completion.

7. Completion

The buyer's money reaches your conveyancer, your mortgage is paid off, and the keys are released. Leave the property empty, clean and with everything agreed on the TA10.

8. After completion

  • Your conveyancer pays the estate agent and sends you the balance.
  • If the property wasn't your main home, you may owe Capital Gains Tax, which must be reported and paid within 60 days.

What it costs

Seller conveyancing fees are commonly a few hundred pounds up to £1,500+ plus VAT and disbursements, depending on the property and whether it's leasehold. Always get a fixed-fee quote in writing.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to sell a house legally?

Usually 12–20 weeks from offer to completion, and longer for leasehold or long chains.

What forms does a seller fill in?

The TA6 and TA10, plus the TA7 if leasehold. Answer honestly, and use "not known" if you're unsure.

When is a house sale legally binding?

Only at exchange of contracts. Before that, either side can withdraw.

Can I do my own conveyancing as a seller?

It's legal, but if you have a mortgage your lender needs to be repaid through a professional, and many buyers' solicitors are reluctant to deal with unrepresented sellers. Most sellers use a conveyancer and do the forms themselves.
