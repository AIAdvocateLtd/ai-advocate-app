Small claims court and consumer refunds: how to get your money back

Faulty product, a builder who didn't finish, or a company ignoring you? Most disputes can be sorted without a lawyer. Here's how.

Last reviewed: October 2026 · Covers England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland
Quick answer: under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 you can reject faulty goods for a full refund within 30 days. If you paid by card, ask your bank for a chargeback or use Section 75 (credit cards, £100–£30,000). If the trader still won't pay, send a letter before claim, then make a small claim — online in England and Wales for claims up to £10,000.

Your rights when you buy goods

Goods must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose and as described.

  • Within 30 days: you can reject faulty goods and get a full refund.
  • After 30 days (up to 6 months): the seller gets one chance to repair or replace. If that fails, you can ask for a refund (possibly reduced for use) or a price reduction. In the first 6 months it's assumed the fault was there at the start unless the seller proves otherwise.
  • After 6 months: you can still claim, but you may need to show the fault was there from the start. You generally have up to 6 years to take legal action (5 in Scotland).

Online and phone purchases also come with a 14-day "change of mind" cancellation period under the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, with some exceptions such as personalised items.

Your rights with services

Services (builders, mechanics, cleaners and so on) must be carried out with reasonable care and skill, within a reasonable time, and for a reasonable price if none was agreed. If the work is poor, you can ask the trader to put it right, and if that isn't possible, ask for some or all of your money back.

Getting money back through your card

  • Chargeback: for debit cards, credit cards and some other payments. Ask your bank, usually within 120 days. It's a scheme rule, not a law, but banks use it often.
  • Section 75: if you paid any part of a purchase costing more than £100 and up to £30,000 by credit card, the card company is jointly liable with the seller (Consumer Credit Act 1974).

Step-by-step: from complaint to small claim

  1. Complain in writing, explaining the problem and what you want (refund, repair, compensation). Keep receipts, photos and messages.
  2. Try an ombudsman or ADR scheme if the trader belongs to one (for example energy, telecoms, finance or many trade bodies). It's usually free.
  3. Send a letter before claim giving the trader a final deadline (often 14 days) and saying you'll go to court if they don't pay.
  4. Start a small claim:
    • England & Wales: use Money Claim Online on GOV.UK for claims up to £10,000 (lower limits apply to personal injury and housing disrepair). You pay a court fee, which you can recover if you win.
    • Scotland: use "simple procedure" at the sheriff court for claims up to £5,000.
    • Northern Ireland: use the small claims court for lower-value claims — check nidirect for the current limit.
  5. Mediation: in England and Wales, many small claims now include a free mediation appointment before a hearing.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a full refund for faulty goods?

Yes, if you reject them within 30 days. After that, the seller gets one chance to repair or replace first.

What's the small claims limit?

£10,000 for most claims in England and Wales; £5,000 for simple procedure in Scotland; Northern Ireland has its own limit.

Do I need a solicitor for small claims?

No. The small claims process is designed for people to use themselves, and legal costs are usually not recoverable anyway.

What is Section 75?

It makes your credit card provider equally responsible for purchases over £100 and up to £30,000 if something goes wrong.
