Workplace discrimination in the UK: your rights

Being treated worse, harassed or held back because of who you are is unlawful. Here's what counts, how to prove it and how to take action.

Last reviewed: October 2026 · Equality Act 2010 · Covers England, Wales and Scotland (Northern Ireland has separate laws)
Quick answer: the Equality Act 2010 protects you from day one (even as a job applicant) against discrimination, harassment and victimisation linked to 9 protected characteristics. Raise a written grievance, keep evidence, and contact ACAS early conciliation quickly, because tribunal deadlines are short.

The 9 protected characteristics

  • Age
  • Disability
  • Gender reassignment
  • Marriage and civil partnership
  • Pregnancy and maternity
  • Race (including colour, nationality and ethnic origin)
  • Religion or belief
  • Sex
  • Sexual orientation

Types of discrimination

  • Direct — treated worse because of a characteristic (e.g. not promoted because of your age).
  • Indirect — a rule that applies to everyone but disadvantages a group, without good justification.
  • Harassment — unwanted conduct that violates your dignity or creates a hostile environment, including "banter". Employers must also take reasonable steps to prevent sexual harassment.
  • Victimisation — punished for complaining about discrimination or supporting someone who did.
  • Failure to make reasonable adjustments — for disabled workers, such as changed hours, equipment or duties.

Building your evidence

  • Keep a dated diary of every incident: who, what, where and witnesses.
  • Save emails, messages, appraisals and rotas.
  • Note how others without your characteristic were treated.
  • You can send a written question to your employer asking them to explain their treatment of you.

Steps to take

  1. Raise a formal written grievance.
  2. Get advice from ACAS or the Equality Advisory Support Service.
  3. Contact ACAS early conciliation within the time limit, even if your grievance is ongoing.
  4. If it isn't resolved, submit an ET1 claim to the employment tribunal.

What you can get

Compensation for lost earnings has no cap, plus an award for injury to feelings. Discrimination cases may also qualify for Legal Aid.

Frequently asked questions

What are the protected characteristics?

Age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage and civil partnership, pregnancy and maternity, race, religion or belief, sex, and sexual orientation.

Do I need 2 years' service?

No. Protection applies from day one, including to job applicants.

What's the time limit?

It's short, and it's being extended from 3 to 6 months under the Employment Rights Act 2025. Contact ACAS early conciliation as soon as possible.

Can I be sacked for complaining?

No. That's victimisation and is itself unlawful.
