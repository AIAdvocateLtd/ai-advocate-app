Landlord won't return your deposit? Here's what to do

Your deposit is your money. Landlords can only keep part of it for genuine reasons, and there's a free way to challenge them.

Last reviewed: October 2026 · Covers England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland
Quick answer: most tenancy deposits must be protected in a government-approved scheme. Ask for your deposit back in writing; if your landlord makes unfair deductions, use the scheme's free dispute service. If your deposit was never protected, you may be able to claim up to 3 times the deposit as compensation.

Step 1: check whether your deposit was protected

Search for your deposit on each scheme's website using your name, postcode and tenancy start date:

  • England & Wales: Deposit Protection Service (DPS), mydeposits, or Tenancy Deposit Scheme (TDS). Your landlord must protect it within 30 days and give you the "prescribed information".
  • Scotland: SafeDeposits Scotland, Letting Protection Service Scotland, or mydeposits Scotland — within 30 working days of the tenancy starting.
  • Northern Ireland: TDS Northern Ireland or Letting Protection Service NI — within 28 days.

Step 2: ask for it back in writing

At the end of your tenancy, email or write to your landlord or agent asking for the full deposit, giving your bank details and a date to reply by. Keep a copy. In England, once you've agreed the amount, the deposit must be repaid within 10 days.

Step 3: challenge unfair deductions

A landlord can deduct for unpaid rent, damage beyond fair wear and tear, missing items, or cleaning if the property is dirtier than when you moved in. They can't charge for normal wear and tear, like faded paint or worn carpets after years of use. They should back up deductions with evidence, such as the check-in inventory, photos and quotes.

If you disagree, raise a dispute with the deposit scheme. Their adjudication service is free, impartial and usually quicker than court. Send your evidence: check-in and check-out reports, dated photos and messages.

Step 4: if your deposit wasn't protected

  • England & Wales: you can apply to the county court. The court must order the landlord to pay you between 1 and 3 times the deposit, as well as return it (Housing Act 2004).
  • Scotland: apply to the First-tier Tribunal (Housing and Property Chamber) within 3 months of the tenancy ending. It can award up to 3 times the deposit.
  • Northern Ireland: failing to protect a deposit is an offence, and the council can fine the landlord.

Other useful rules

  • In England, deposits for most tenancies are capped at 5 weeks' rent (or 6 weeks if annual rent is £50,000 or more) under the Tenant Fees Act 2019.
  • Holding deposits are capped at 1 week's rent and must normally be refunded or used towards rent.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a landlord have to return my deposit?

In England, within 10 days of agreeing the amount. If you disagree, use the scheme's free dispute service.

What if my deposit was never protected?

In England and Wales a court can order the landlord to pay you 1–3 times the deposit on top of returning it. Scotland has a similar penalty through the tribunal.

Can they charge for wear and tear?

No — only damage beyond fair wear and tear, unpaid rent, missing items or excessive dirt, backed by evidence.

My landlord kept it for "cleaning" — is that allowed?

Only if the property is less clean than when you moved in. Your check-in inventory and photos are key evidence.
